The print is the Springer typescript of SGA 4½ (P. Deligne, Cohomologie étale, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 569, 1977); the page number in the file names is Springer's number at the foot of the page, and the running head at the top names the part (Arcata, Rapport, Fonction L mod. l^n, Dualité, Sommes trig., Th. finitude) with its own page number. The TeX is NOT a diplomatic transcription: it is the LaTeX rendition of SGA 4½ by Daniel Miller, which its typesetter's note calls an essentially unmodified version of the original, as held in the corpus (read only). Its own form is therefore not an error: layout, line breaks, formulas set as displays or run into the text, diagrams redrawn with xymatrix; numbering style and the typography of headings and statements (LaTeX numbers sections, paragraphs and statements itself, so their printed numbers are not in the source: the \label name carries the number, II:4-5 being 4.5 of the Rapport and I:4-3-4 being (3.4) of Arcata IV, and \tag{...} gives a formula's number; compare those with the print); emphasis (the typescript's underlining set in italics or not at all, and italics the print does not have); modern spacing and quotation marks, accented capitals; and macro choices for the same sign (\dZ, \dF, \dG ... double-struck, \sF, \sG ... script, \cA ... calligraphic, \fm ... Fraktur, \eR and \eD sans-serif R and D, \h for H, \iso for an isomorphism arrow, operator names in lower case such as tr, ker, im, hom, gr for the print's Tr, Ker, Im, Hom, Gr; the definitions are in the edition's sga-style.sty, named below, if one must be looked up). The typesetter's stated conventions, not to be itemized (say in your message, in one line, whether your pages follow them): script letters for sheaves where the print has roman ones, and sheaf and ordinary functors told apart by macro (\Hom, \Ext, \Tor for the sheaf versions, \hom, \ext, \tor for the ordinary ones, \rHom for the derived sheaf Hom, \rhom for the derived ordinary Hom) where the print uses one notation; report a place where the TeX's choice contradicts a distinction the print makes. Report only differences of content: words; numbers (of statements, paragraphs and formulas, and in references); formulas, indices and exponents; symbols and letters (another letter, or another alphabet where the print tells objects apart by it); punctuation that changes the meaning; missing or extra text (words, lines, formulas, footnotes, arrows and labels of diagrams); wrong references. References go through labels: \ref{II:4-5} prints the number in the label's name, \eqref adds parentheses, \hyperref[IV]{Cycle} prints Cycle, and \cite[IX 2.10]{sga4} stands for the print's bibliographic reference (the keys are in sga-sources.bib): compare the reference with the print (brackets or parentheses around it are style). Comments after % belong to the typesetter, not to the print: do not report them. Misprints of the print, whether the TeX keeps them or corrects them: kind misprint_in_print, "fix": null, and say in the note whether the TeX keeps or corrects them; they are not errors of the TeX. Any other change (another word, a formula rewritten, a clause dropped or added) is a difference of content: report it.
